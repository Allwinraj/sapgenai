Cherry

How Cherry works · one picture

A person describes a pain. Cherry names the process, checks the facts, scores whether it is worth doing, then points at a place to build. It does not run the solution. One example all the way through: suppliers go dark and the team hears too late.

1. The conversation

Eight steps. Cherry can loop. It cannot skip “is it worth it?” and jump to a product.

1
Listen

“I’m procurement. Suppliers go dark. We hear late.”

2
Name it

Industry = manufacturing. Role = director. Process = supplier monitoring.

3
Find the gap

Monitoring has no real control. Today it is Excel and mail.

4
Check facts

Their numbers plus related benchmarks. Lag they did not confirm stays a guess.

5
Score ROI

Value graph runs the calculation. Thin / real / material. No product yet.

6
Give options

Solution graph starts: watchlist, live signal, pack.

7
Give the solution

Last graph names the door: SAP, Joule, EY, Nexus, or custom.

8
Sign off

The director confirms. That freeze is the blueprint. Cherry stops.

2. The six graphs

Process sits in the middle. Industry and Persona are fed catalogs. Evidence brings facts and related benchmarks. Value is the ROI. Solution is last — it gives the answer, it does not run it.

Industry · fed knowledge graph

We feed Cherry an industry KG (chains, typical processes, pools, regulation). Chat only resolves this client onto it.

Manufacturing KG → supplier monitoring and visibility pool are allowed.

Process · context hub

Not only a name. Context around the depot ID: as-is vs expected, systems, volume, plants, how it actually runs here.

Supplier monitoring · no control · Excel + mail · this plant.

Persona · linked ontology

Roles linked to tasks, decisions, authority, and KPIs. Not a single job title.

Director → owns monitoring → KPI days-to-see-risk → may sign. Analyst cannot.

name it on the catalogs · hang this client’s context on Process

Evidence · facts + benchmarks

Case facts (their files) plus related industry/peer benchmarks for this use case. A benchmark is shown; it does not silently become their number.

Fact: 38% spend in top 20. Benchmark: peers see risk in X days. Their lag = still ask.

Value · ROI calculation

This graph is the ROI engine: pool, lever, KPI, drivers, formula, band. Uses facts (and optional benchmark as comparison). Does not pick a product.

ROI: visibility lag × concentrated spend → MATERIAL. You vs peer shown. No door yet.

Solution · last graph

After ROI, this graph creates and gives the solution: what to do and which door. Cherry does not build or run it.

Give: live-signal capability. Door: SAP for spend, compose the rest, no EY hit.

3. Why these graphs exist

Cherry is not a chat log. Each graph is a job. If you drop one, a later step lies.

Industry

What it is
A knowledge graph we feed Cherry: sectors, value chains, typical processes, typical pools, regulation. The case only points at a node in that KG.
Why Cherry needs it
So “supplier monitoring” and a “visibility” pool are allowed in this industry — not copied from another sector’s story.
Impact
With a fed KG, scoring stays in bounds. Without it, Cherry invents work this company does not have.
If missing
Stop and ask. Do not grow a new industry inside the chat.

Persona

What it is
A linked ontology of roles: role → tasks → decisions → authority → KPIs they care about → other roles (director vs analyst vs controller).
Why Cherry needs it
ROI is the director’s “days to see risk,” not generic hours. Sign-off is a link on the ontology, not anyone with a browser.
Impact
With links, Cherry scores the right KPI and only a real owner can freeze. Without them, an analyst confirms and IT tickets get treated as value.
If missing
Keep mapping who owns the pain. Do not run ROI on a generic “user.”

Process · the centre

What it is
A context graph on a depot ID: expected control, as-is (Excel, mail, SAP), systems, volume, plants, exceptions — how it actually runs here.
Why Cherry needs it
Industry, Persona, Evidence, ROI levers, and Solution all attach to this process-in-context. A slogan is not enough.
Impact
With context, Diagnose has a gap and ROI knows what can change. Without it, every later graph is theatre.
If missing
Do not run ROI. Do not give a solution. Name the process and its as-is first.

Evidence

What it is
Two kinds of node: case facts (their files, confirmed numbers) and related benchmarks (peer / industry norms for this use case). Each has a source.
Why Cherry needs it
ROI needs numbers. Cherry should also show related benchmarks. A benchmark must not silently replace a missing case number.
Impact
With both, ROI can say “you vs peer.” Without case facts, MATERIAL is a guess. Without benchmarks, the case has no comparison.
If missing
Ask for their number. You may show a peer lag labelled “benchmark, not their fact.”

Value

What it is
The ROI calculation: pool → lever → KPI → driver → formula → band. Optional “you vs benchmark.” Solution is not an input.
Why Cherry needs it
This is how Cherry says “worth doing” without selling a platform. Industry KG, persona ontology, process context, and Evidence all feed ROI.
Impact
Thin ROI dies here. Material ROI is allowed to go to Solution. Without this graph, every workshop is a product pitch.
If missing
No path from process to formula means no ROI paragraph.

Solution

What it is
The last graph. After ROI it creates and gives the solution: capabilities plus a door (SAP, Joule, EY asset, Nexus, custom). It does not run the solution.
Why Cherry needs it
A material ROI still needs an answer. This is recommend, not build. Asset registry is checked here.
Impact
With it, the director leaves with a given solution and honest why-nots. Too early = a commercial. Never = ROI with nowhere to go.
If missing after material ROI
Cherry failed its last job. If it fills during ROI, Cherry failed its first job.

4. How ROI uses the graphs

Value is the ROI calculation. Fixed order. Miss a hop, do not score. Solution is not on this row.

Industry KG

Is a visibility pool even in the manufacturing graph? Yes.

Persona ontology

Whose KPI is linked to this role? Days to see risk — not IT tickets.

Process context

What can change in how it runs here? Add a live signal — no control today.

Evidence

Case fact: concentration. Related benchmark: peer days. Their lag — ask, or label benchmark.

ROI → band

Formula on allowed numbers. You vs peer. Material. Still no door.

“ROI is material: you cannot see supplier risk in time, spend is concentrated, and peers see it faster. That is why it is worth doing. We have not given a solution yet.” If they refuse that sentence, Cherry goes back to ROI. It does not invent a product.

5. Last graph — give the solution

Solution turns on after ROI. It creates the recommendation and names a door. Cherry does not go inside.

Create what could help

Watchlist. Continuous signal. A pack for the director. If the EY registry has this asset, include it. If not, do not pretend.

Give the door

SAP · Joule · EY asset · Nexus · custom. Example: spend stays in SAP, the signal is composed, not Joule, no EY hit. That is the given solution.

Cherry is fed industry and persona knowledge. It hangs this client’s process context, checks facts and related benchmarks, runs ROI, then the last graph gives a solution. The signed picture is the blueprint. Running the solution is not Cherry.
